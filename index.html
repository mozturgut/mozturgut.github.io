About

I use single-cell data to understand how the immune system responds to spinal cord injury.

I am a PhD student in the McCreedy Lab at Texas A&M University. My work is computational: I build R pipelines that take single-cell RNA sequencing data from the injured spinal cord, from raw counts through cell type identification and down to the signals that cells send each other.

A normal RNA-seq experiment gives you the average of a whole tissue. Single-cell RNA-seq measures thousands of cells one by one, so we can see which immune cells show up after an injury, what state they are in, and how that changes over the first days after injury.

Research focus

Immune cell states and signaling after spinal cord injury

After a spinal cord injury, neutrophils and other immune cells flood into the tissue within hours. Some of these cells help damage spread, and others may help repair. I study how these cells differ from each other, and what signals from neighboring cells push them toward one state or another.

To do this I combine and re-analyze several published mouse spinal cord injury scRNA-seq datasets across early time points after injury, and compare results from several cell-cell communication methods to find signals that hold up across datasets and tools.

How I analyze data

My scRNA-seq workflow

Every project follows the same steps. Each step is its own commented, linear R script, so anyone in the lab can re-run it from start to end.

Quality controlRemove empty droplets, dying cells and doubletsSeurat
IntegrationNormalize and merge datasets, remove batch effectsNormalizeData
Cell typesCluster cells, UMAP, name clusters by marker genesFindClusters
Differential expressionGenes that change between groups and time pointsFindMarkers
CommunicationLigand–receptor signals between cell typesCellChat
PathwaysPathway activity and upstream regulatorsdecoupleR
