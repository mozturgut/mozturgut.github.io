About Me

Bridging neuroscience, immunology, and computational biology to understand recovery after spinal cord injury

I am a PhD candidate in Biology at Texas A&M University, working under the mentorship of Dr. Dylan A. McCreedy. My research sits at the intersection of neuroimmunology, single-cell genomics, and computational biology.

My dissertation focuses on how neutrophil depletion and hormonal manipulation reveal sex-dependent immune remodeling after spinal cord injury, analyzed through single-cell RNA sequencing. I have developed standardized computational workflows to integrate and analyze scRNA-seq data across multiple independent published datasets, identifying reparative neutrophil phenotypes and characterizing signaling pathways associated with immune resolution.

Research

My research spans from computational single-cell genomics to bench-level neuroscience, investigating the immune system's role in neural recovery

Single-Cell RNA Sequencing

Developing and applying Seurat-based computational pipelines for quality control, batch correction, dimensionality reduction, cell type annotation, and differential expression analysis across multi-dataset integrations.

scRNA-seq Seurat UMAP

Neuroimmunology

Investigating how neutrophils regulate immune resolution after spinal cord injury in a sex- and time-dependent manner. Identified reparative neutrophil phenotypes expressing anti-inflammatory markers (Arg1+, CD206+, Lgals1+).

SCI Neutrophils Immune Resolution

Ligand-Receptor Networks

Characterizing cell-cell communication pathways and signaling networks associated with immune resolution through CellChat and CellCall ligand-receptor-TF analysis frameworks.

CellChat CellCall Signaling

Circadian Neurobiology

Discovered that the Clock (Clk) transcription factor is required for maintenance of small ventral lateral neurons (sLNvs), master pacemaker neurons in Drosophila. Loss of sLNvs is associated with apoptosis during metamorphosis.

Drosophila Circadian Clk

Behavioral Neuroscience

Studied negative contrast and associative learning in Apis mellifera (honeybees), examining how individual foraging decisions translate into colony-level recruitment behavior following reward downshifts.

Honeybees Learning Behavior

Molecular Genetics

Conducted molecular genetics research using PCR, DNA extraction, immunostaining, and confocal microscopy techniques for species-level genetic identification and neurobiology studies.

PCR Confocal Immunostaining
